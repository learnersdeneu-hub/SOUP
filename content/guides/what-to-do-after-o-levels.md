# What to Do After O-Levels or IGCSEs: Choosing the Right Route to University

Finishing your O-levels or IGCSEs is the first real fork in the road to university. The choice you make now, whether that's A-levels, the IB, a national qualification such as FSc, or something else, shapes **which universities you can apply to, in which countries, and for which degrees**.

This guide is for students in **Pakistan, the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar and across the Gulf**, and for their parents. It explains the main routes and the equivalence rules that matter in your region, and how to choose subjects without closing doors you may want later.

> **Last reviewed:** 18 November 2026. Equivalence rules and entry requirements change. Confirm on the official sources linked at the end.

## Option 1: A-levels

**A-levels** are the most common choice for O-level and IGCSE students who are aiming for university abroad.

- **What they are:** usually **three subjects** (sometimes four) studied in depth over two years, offered by Cambridge International or Pearson Edexcel.
- **Where they lead:** accepted for direct entry by universities across the **UK, Europe, the US, the GCC and Pakistan**, subject to each university's grade and subject requirements.
- **Best for:** students who know roughly which field they want, and who do well with focused, exam-based study.

Three A-levels is the benchmark most universities work from. Germany, for example, now asks for **three A-levels in distinct subjects** and no longer counts AS levels. [See how European universities assess A-levels →](/guides/study-in-europe-after-a-levels)

## Option 2: The IB Diploma

The **International Baccalaureate Diploma** is a two-year programme of **six subjects** plus a core (Theory of Knowledge, the Extended Essay and CAS).

- **Strengths:** breadth, strong writing and research skills, and wide international recognition.
- **Trade-off:** a heavier, broader workload. It suits students who like range more than depth.

## Option 3: A national route (for example FSc / FA in Pakistan)

Some students switch from O-levels to a national intermediate qualification such as **FSc (pre-medical or pre-engineering)**.

- **Strengths:** closely matched to local entrance tests and admissions, and usually lower cost.
- **Trade-off:** international universities assess national qualifications individually, and some require an additional year (a foundation year or first-year university study) before bachelor's entry.

If you're certain you'll study at home, a national route can make sense. If you're aiming abroad, A-levels or the IB usually keep more doors open.

## Option 4: A foundation route

**Foundation years** are one-year programmes, often run by or with a university, that lead into the **first year of a bachelor's degree**. They're most useful when your qualifications don't meet a country's direct-entry rules, for example where fewer than 12 years of schooling don't meet Italy's requirement, or when you want to switch subject area.

## Equivalence: the rules that matter in your region

### Pakistan: IBCC equivalence

If you plan to study at a Pakistani university, you will need an **equivalence certificate from the Inter Board Committee of Chairmen (IBCC)**.

- Students who took O-levels **in Pakistan** generally need **eight O-level subjects**: five compulsory (English, Mathematics, Urdu, Islamiyat and Pakistan Studies) plus three electives.
- Students who studied **overseas** may have lighter requirements, and some may be exempt from Urdu, Islamiyat and Pakistan Studies. The rules depend on nationality and where you sat each exam.
- For **A-levels**, IBCC typically expects **three A-level subjects**, and **medical and engineering** routes require the relevant sciences.

**Action:** if there's any chance you'll apply in Pakistan, check your O-level subject list against IBCC's rules **before** you finish, not after.

### UAE: Ministry of Education equivalency

For universities in the UAE, students from British-curriculum schools usually need an **equivalency certificate** from the UAE Ministry of Education. Common requirements include:

- a minimum number of **IGCSE/O-level subjects**, often five, with grades A*–E or 9–3;
- plus **AS/A-level subjects**. Several universities ask for more than the ministry minimum. Mohammed bin Zayed University, for example, lists five O-level and three AS/A-level subjects at grade C or above.
- Arabic and Islamic Education may **not** count towards the subject total at some universities.

Requirements vary by university and programme, especially for medicine, pharmacy and engineering, so check each university's own admissions page.

### Saudi Arabia, Qatar and the wider GCC

Each country has its own equivalency process for foreign-curriculum certificates. If you may study in your home country later, ask your school which subjects and how many are needed for local equivalency **before** you choose your A-levels.

## How to choose A-level subjects

1. **Start from the degree, not the subject.** Medicine, engineering and computer science have firm subject requirements. Business, law and the humanities are usually more flexible. [See A-level requirements by degree →](/guides/a-level-subjects-for-medicine-engineering-business-law)
2. **Keep Mathematics if you can.** It's required or strongly preferred for engineering, computer science, economics and many business programmes, in the UK, Europe and elsewhere.
3. **Check country-specific rules.** For example, Germany's rule that A-levels must be in distinct subjects, and Pakistan's science requirements for medical and engineering admission.
4. **Play to your strengths.** Strong grades in suitable subjects beat weak grades in "prestigious" ones.
5. **Think about the timeline.** UK medicine and Oxford/Cambridge applications close in **October** of your final year, so you'll apply with **predicted grades**. [How predicted grades work →](/guides/applying-to-university-with-predicted-grades)

## Frequently asked questions

### What should I do after O-levels?

Most students aiming for university abroad take A-levels or the IB Diploma, both of which allow direct entry to universities in the UK, Europe, the US and the GCC. A national route such as FSc suits students focused on home-country universities. A foundation year bridges gaps in qualifications.

### Are A-levels better than FSc?

Neither is better in general. A-levels are more widely accepted for direct entry abroad. FSc is closely aligned with Pakistani university entrance and is usually cheaper. Choose based on where you're most likely to study.

### How many O-levels do I need for IBCC equivalence?

Students who studied in Pakistan generally need eight O-level subjects, including English, Mathematics, Urdu, Islamiyat and Pakistan Studies, plus three electives. Overseas students may have different requirements. Check with IBCC.

### Do I need equivalency to study at a UAE university with A-levels?

Generally, yes. UAE universities usually require an equivalency certificate from the UAE Ministry of Education for British-curriculum qualifications, plus their own subject and grade requirements.
