# Studying in Europe After A-Levels: What Universities Actually Require

Every year, thousands of A-level students in Lahore, Karachi, Dubai, Riyadh, Nairobi and Kuala Lumpur ask the same question: *"Can I go straight to a European university with my A-levels, or do I need a foundation year?"*

The honest answer is that **it depends on the country, the type of university, and your exact subjects and grades**. European countries do not all treat A-levels the same way. This guide explains how four popular destinations, **Germany, the Netherlands, Italy and Greece**, assess A-level students, and what to do if you don't meet the requirements directly.

> **Last reviewed:** 17 November 2026. Admission rules change, and each university can add its own conditions. Use this guide to plan, then confirm the details on the official sources linked below.

## The big idea: equivalence

Most continental European universities don't ask, "Did you take A-levels?" They ask, **"Is your school qualification equivalent to our own university-entrance qualification?"** That means the German *Abitur*, the Dutch *VWO* diploma, the Italian *diploma di maturità*, and so on.

Whether your A-levels count as equivalent usually depends on:

- **how many full A-levels** you have (AS levels often don't count);
- **which subjects** they are, since some countries need subjects from specific groups;
- **your grades**;
- sometimes **your GCSEs/IGCSEs**, which can count alongside your A-levels.

Cambridge International A-levels and Pearson Edexcel International A-levels are generally treated in the same way as UK A-levels. Check the specific country's guidance if your school offers a different board.

## Germany

Germany is attractive because most public universities charge **no tuition fees** for most bachelor's programmes. Baden-Württemberg is a notable exception for non-EU students. Germany is also one of the most precise countries about A-level equivalence.

**What the DAAD and uni-assist guidance says:**

- Since 2022, the standard requirement is **three A-levels**, replacing the older "three A-levels plus one AS level" rule.
- **AS levels are no longer taken into account** for university admission.
- Your A-levels should be in **distinct subjects**. Guidance notes that Mathematics and Further Mathematics count as one subject for this purpose.
- Some fields have **subject restrictions**. For example, mathematics and science degrees may require relevant A-levels, while arts, humanities and law no longer require mathematics.
- Each university can set **additional requirements and grade thresholds**, and many publish them on their own admissions pages.

**Language matters more than you think.** Most German bachelor's programmes are taught in German. English-taught bachelor's programmes exist but are a minority, especially at public universities. Many more programmes are taught in English at master's level.

**Money for the visa.** Non-EU students usually need to show proof of funds, often through a blocked account. Commercial providers report the 2026 requirement as **€992 per month (€11,904 a year)**, linked to the national student grant rate. Check the current figure with the German mission in your country before you apply.

**If you don't qualify directly:** Germany's *Studienkolleg* is a one-year preparatory course that leads to the *Feststellungsprüfung* assessment exam. It is usually taught in German.

## The Netherlands

Dutch universities teach a large number of bachelor's programmes in English, which makes the Netherlands popular with international A-level students.

There are two kinds of institution, and they have different entry levels:

- **Research universities (*universiteiten*, WO)** expect a qualification equivalent to the Dutch **VWO** diploma. A commonly quoted benchmark is **three A-levels**, often with minimum grades and sometimes specific subjects.
- **Universities of applied sciences (*hogescholen*, HBO)** are more practical and career-focused. They often have **lower entry requirements**, and some guidance suggests two A-levels for certain programmes.

**An example of a published rule:** Maastricht University's international diploma requirements ask for **three A-levels in academic subjects at grades A–C, plus three GCSEs in other subjects**. They also state that AS levels cannot make up for missing A-levels.

**Practical points:**

- Many programmes require **Mathematics** at a set level, as well as English. Check the programme page carefully.
- Applications usually start on **Studielink**, the national registration system, followed by the university's own process.
- Deadlines vary. Some selective programmes close as early as **15 January**, while others have later deadlines, often 1 April or 1 May for non-EU applicants.

## Italy

Italy's public universities have relatively low fees, often linked to family income, and a growing number of English-taught programmes.

**The 12-year rule.** Italian universities require non-EU applicants to hold a secondary school diploma obtained after **at least 12 years of schooling**. For most A-level students this is satisfied, but check how your system's years are counted.

**If you have fewer than 12 years**, some universities, such as Sapienza University of Rome, accept proof of completed university study to make up the gap. Others, such as Parma, offer a **foundation year** for this purpose.

**Universitaly pre-enrolment.** Non-EU students who need a study visa must **pre-enrol on the Universitaly portal** for their chosen programme. The visa application at the Italian embassy or consulate follows from that pre-enrolment summary. Each university also runs its own admissions assessment, and some programmes require entrance tests such as **TOLC** or **IMAT** (for medicine).

**Deadlines vary by university and programme.** For example, published 2026 pre-enrolment deadlines range from late summer to autumn. Limited-access programmes close much earlier.

## Greece

Greece is the newest of these four destinations for many international students. Public universities now offer **English-taught programmes for international applicants**, through the official **Study in Greece** platform.

- The Study in Greece helpdesk states that **most undergraduate programmes accept A-levels** (along with the IB, AP/SAT and European/French Baccalaureate) as equivalent to a high-school diploma.
- International applicants to English-taught programmes **do not sit the Greek Panhellenic exams**.
- English-taught bachelor's options are still a relatively small group, mostly in medicine, business, economics and the humanities. There are many more English-taught master's programmes.

## What if I don't meet the requirements?

Falling short in one country does not mean falling short everywhere. Common routes include:

1. **Choose a different country or type of university.** For example, a Dutch university of applied sciences instead of a research university.
2. **A foundation year**, run by a university or a recognised provider, which leads to entry to the first year of a bachelor's degree.
3. **Retake or add an A-level.** One extra full A-level can sometimes make the difference in Germany.
4. **Start in a related programme** with lower entry requirements and transfer later, where the university allows it.

## Planning timeline for A-level students

- **Year 12 (AS year):** shortlist countries, check which subjects each requires, and plan an English test (IELTS/TOEFL) if needed.
- **Summer before Year 13:** prepare documents, including your passport, transcripts and predicted grades, and draft a motivation letter.
- **Autumn–winter of Year 13:** apply to early-deadline programmes (some Dutch programmes close in January) and register on the national portals.
- **Spring of Year 13:** apply to later-deadline programmes, then start planning your visa and finances.
- **Results day:** send final grades and meet any conditions on your offers.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I study in Europe with A-levels?

Yes. A-levels are widely accepted across Europe, but each country checks them against its own university-entrance qualification. The number of A-levels, the subjects and the grades all matter.

### How many A-levels do I need for Germany?

Current DAAD guidance says three A-levels in distinct subjects. AS levels are no longer counted. Some fields have subject restrictions, and universities can add their own requirements.

### Do AS levels count in Europe?

Often not. Germany no longer counts AS levels, and Maastricht University states that AS levels cannot compensate for missing A-levels. Check each university's rules.

### Can I apply with predicted grades?

Many European universities accept applications with predicted grades and make conditional offers. Others only assess you once final results are available. Check each programme's process.
