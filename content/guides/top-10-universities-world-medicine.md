# Top 10 Universities in the World for Medicine

Medicine is the most competitive degree in the world to get into, and the most rule-bound. This guide lists the **top 10 universities for medicine** in the latest **QS World University Rankings by Subject (2026)**, then explains what really matters for an international applicant: **whether you can apply as a school leaver, how many international places exist, and what it costs**.

> **Ranking used:** QS World University Rankings by Subject 2026, Medicine (released March 2026). Rankings measure research reputation and output, not teaching quality or your chances of admission. **Last reviewed:** 18 November 2026.

## What this means for international applicants

### The UK four (Oxford, Cambridge, UCL, Imperial): apply straight from school

Oxford, Cambridge, UCL and Imperial offer **undergraduate medicine** that you apply to **straight after A-levels or the IB**, through **UCAS**.

- **Deadline:** for 2027 entry, **15 October 2026** (18:00 UK time), with predicted grades. [How predicted grades work →](/guides/applying-to-university-with-predicted-grades)
- **Admissions test:** all four now use the **UCAT**. The BMAT has been discontinued.
- **Subjects:** Chemistry, and usually Biology, plus strong grades across the board. [A-level requirements for medicine →](/guides/a-level-subjects-for-medicine-engineering-business-law)
- **International places:** UK medical schools have a **limited, capped number of places** for international students, so competition is intense and fees are high.

### The US five (Harvard, Stanford, Johns Hopkins, UCSF, Yale): medicine comes after a bachelor's

In the US, medicine is a **graduate degree (MD)** taken **after a four-year bachelor's degree**, usually with pre-med science courses. International applicants are considered, but places for non-US citizens are **very limited**, and financial aid for internationals varies by school. For most A-level students, the realistic route is a strong bachelor's degree first, in the US or elsewhere.

### Karolinska Institutet (Sweden): world-class, but Swedish-taught

Karolinska's medical degree is taught in **Swedish**, so most international students meet Karolinska through its **English-taught master's programmes** and research.

## Strong English-taught medicine beyond the top 10

The top 10 is only one part of the picture. Many students from Pakistan and the GCC study medicine in **English** at well-regarded public universities elsewhere, often for much less. For example:

- **Greece:** the National and Kapodistrian University of Athens lists an English-taught medical degree for international students. [Study in Greece guide →](/guides/study-in-greece-english-taught-programmes)
- **Italy:** several public universities teach medicine in English, usually through the **IMAT** admissions test. [Studying in Europe after A-levels →](/guides/study-in-europe-after-a-levels)

**Check recognition first.** If you plan to practise in **Pakistan**, the **GCC** or the **UK**, check how your future regulator treats a degree from that country before you apply.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which university is the best in the world for medicine?

Harvard University is first for medicine in the QS World University Rankings by Subject 2026, followed by Oxford, Stanford and Johns Hopkins.

### Can international students study medicine at Oxford or Cambridge?

Yes. Both accept international applicants through UCAS for undergraduate medicine, with a limited number of international places. Applications close on 15 October for the following year's entry, and both require the UCAT.

### Can I study medicine in the US straight after A-levels?

No. In the US, medicine is a graduate (MD) degree taken after a bachelor's degree. A-level students would first complete an undergraduate degree, usually with pre-med science courses.

### Is a top-ranked university necessary to become a good doctor?

No. Rankings mostly reflect research. What matters for your career is a recognised degree, strong clinical training, and licensing in the country where you want to practise.
