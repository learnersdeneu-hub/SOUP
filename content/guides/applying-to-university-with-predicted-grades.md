# Applying to University With Predicted Grades: How It Works and What Happens Next

Most A-level students apply to university **before** they have their final results. Universities make decisions on your **predicted grades**, often with a **conditional offer** that you confirm on results day.

That system works well, but only if you understand it. This guide explains how predicted grades work for **UCAS (UK)**, the **Common App (US)** and **European universities**, what conditional offers mean, and what to do if your results don't go to plan.

> **Last reviewed:** 18 November 2026. Deadlines are for **2027 entry**. Always confirm on the official sites linked below.

## What are predicted grades?

Predicted grades are your school's professional judgement of the grades you're **likely to achieve** in your final A-level exams. Your teachers set them, usually based on AS results, mock exams and your coursework.

## The UK: UCAS

### How predicted grades reach universities

Your school, which UCAS calls your **centre**, is responsible for:

- adding your **predicted grades** to your application;
- writing and approving your **reference**;
- checking and **approving your application** before it goes to UCAS.

You **can't see** your predicted grades or reference in your own application. Once your school sends the application, UCAS **can't change** the reference or predicted grades.

### Key dates for 2027 entry

- **15 October 2026, 18:00 UK time:** deadline for **Oxford and Cambridge** and **most medicine, dentistry and veterinary courses**.
- **January 2027:** the main **equal-consideration deadline** for most other courses. Published guidance gives **13 January 2027 at 18:00 UK time**. Confirm on UCAS.
- Your school will usually set an **internal deadline** weeks earlier, so it has time to add references and predictions.

### Conditional and unconditional offers

- A **conditional offer** sets the grades you must achieve, for example **AAB**, sometimes in specific subjects.
- An **unconditional offer** means you already meet the requirements, for example if you've finished your A-levels.
- You choose a **firm** choice and an **insurance** choice, usually one with lower grade requirements.

### Results day

Cambridge International and Pearson Edexcel release summer-series results in **August**. Universities then **confirm** your place if you've met the conditions.

**If you miss your grades:**

- Your firm or insurance university may **still accept you**, especially if you're close.
- You can look for places through **Clearing**.
- You can ask your school about a **review of marking** if a grade looks wrong.
- You can **retake** and reapply the following year.

## The US: the Common App

US universities don't use predicted grades the way UCAS does. They look at:

- your **school transcript** (O-level/IGCSE and AS results, plus school grades);
- your **counsellor's school report** and **recommendation**;
- **teacher recommendations**;
- **SAT/ACT** scores, where required or submitted;
- essays and activities.

Many US universities accept **predicted A-level grades** as part of the school report. Final results are sent later, and a **mid-year report** and **final report** usually follow from your counsellor. An offer can be withdrawn if your final grades fall a long way below what was reported.

## Europe

Approaches vary by country:

- Many universities make **conditional offers on predicted or provisional grades**, then confirm with final certificates.
- Some, such as Italian universities assessing the **12-year schooling rule**, need your final diploma before **enrolment**, even if they assess you earlier.
- Selective Dutch programmes can close as early as **15 January**, before your final results exist.

## How to get the best predicted grades (honestly)

1. **Treat AS and mock exams seriously.** They're the main evidence teachers use.
2. **Talk to your teachers early.** Ask what you need to show to justify a higher prediction.
3. **Hand in coursework on time** and at your best standard.
4. **Don't apply on the assumption of grades you aren't on track for.** Universities and UCAS track how accurate schools' predictions are.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I apply to university before my A-level results?

Yes. Most students apply with predicted grades and receive conditional offers, which are confirmed when final results arrive in August.

### Who sets my predicted grades?

Your school does, usually your subject teachers, based on AS results, mock exams and coursework. Your school's UCAS centre adds them to your application.

### What happens if I miss my conditional offer?

Your university may still accept you. If not, your insurance choice may. You can also look for places through Clearing, request a review of marking, or reapply after retaking.

### Do US universities accept predicted A-level grades?

Many do, as part of the school report your counsellor submits. Final grades follow later, and offers can be withdrawn if final results are far below what was reported.
