# From Hospitality Diploma to European Degree: English-Taught Tourism and Hospitality Programmes at Public Universities

If you have trained in hospitality, culinary arts or tourism, you already have something many university applicants don't: **practical, industry-ready skills**. The question many diploma graduates ask next is how to turn that experience into a **recognised university degree**, ideally in Europe, where tourism is one of the biggest employers.

This guide covers **English-taught hospitality and tourism programmes at public universities in Greece, Italy, Germany and Portugal**. Each one has been checked against the university's own programme page. The guide also explains honestly which programmes you can realistically enter with which qualifications.

> **Last reviewed:** 17 November 2026. Programme details were checked against official university pages in October 2026. Intakes, fees and requirements change every year, so always confirm on the programme page linked below.

## First, the honest part: which level can you apply for?

"Top-up" can mean different things, so it's worth being precise:

- **Bachelor's programmes** require a **secondary school qualification that meets the country's university-entrance rules**, for example 12 years of schooling for Italy or A-level equivalence for Germany. A hospitality diploma can strengthen your application and show commitment to the field. Whether it counts for **credit or advanced entry** depends entirely on the university's own assessment.
- **Master's programmes** require a **recognised bachelor's degree** (or equivalent), usually in a related field such as hospitality, tourism, business or economics. A diploma on its own is generally **not enough** for master's entry.

So the right route depends on your full academic history, not just your diploma. If you're unsure where you stand, that's exactly the question to bring to a counsellor before you apply.

## Why Europe for hospitality and tourism?

- **Tourism hubs as classrooms.** Programmes in Venice, Rimini, Florence, Athens, the Algarve and Lisbon put you in some of the world's most visited destinations.
- **Public university tuition.** Public universities in these countries generally charge lower fees than private hospitality schools. Most German public universities charge no tuition for most programmes, although the MBA listed below is an exception.
- **Internships and industry links.** Many programmes include placements or project work with tourism organisations.
- **An EU degree** that is recognised across the European Higher Education Area.

## How to choose between them

Ask yourself:

1. **Operations or strategy?** Programmes such as *Hospitality and Tourism Management* lean towards running hotels and tourism businesses. Titles such as *Tourism Futures Studies* or *Planning and Management of Tourism Systems* lean towards destination strategy, policy and research.
2. **Do you want a specialism?** Examples include cruise business (Bremerhaven), health and medical tourism (Deggendorf), sustainability (Harokopio, HNEE, Florence, Leiria) and cultural heritage (Turin).
3. **What do you want your daily life to look like?** You could live in a major city (Athens, Lisbon, Florence) or a smaller university town (Biella, Eberswalde, Pfarrkirchen).
4. **What can you afford?** Tuition varies by country and programme, and living costs vary even more. Check each programme's current fee for non-EU students.
5. **Do you want to work in that country afterwards?** Learning the local language, even alongside an English-taught degree, improves your chances considerably.

## What you'll typically need to apply

- Secondary school certificates and transcripts. For master's applicants, add your **bachelor's degree and transcripts**.
- Your **diploma certificate and transcript** from your hospitality or culinary institute, which is useful supporting evidence.
- **Proof of English**, such as IELTS or TOEFL, unless the programme accepts an exemption.
- A **CV** showing internships, kitchen or hotel placements, and any work experience.
- A **motivation letter** that connects your practical training to the programme.
- **References**, academic or professional.
- **Passport** and, after an offer, **visa, insurance and accommodation** documents.

**Your practical experience is an advantage.** In your motivation letter, be specific about what you did. Describe the service you ran, the menus you costed and the guest problems you solved, and explain what you now want to learn at university level.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I study hospitality in Europe in English?

Yes. Public universities in Greece, Italy, Germany and Portugal offer English-taught hospitality and tourism programmes, mostly at master's level, with some bachelor's options in Italy and Germany.

### Can I apply for a master's with only a hospitality diploma?

Generally, no. Master's programmes require a recognised bachelor's degree or equivalent. With a diploma, you'd usually look at bachelor's programmes, provided your school qualifications meet that country's entry rules.

### Will my diploma give me credit towards a bachelor's degree?

It depends on the university. Some may consider prior learning for credit, while many won't. Ask the admissions office directly before you apply.

### Are these degrees cheaper than private hospitality schools?

Public universities in these countries usually charge considerably less than private international hospitality schools. Most German public universities charge no tuition for most programmes. Always check the current non-EU fee for your programme.
