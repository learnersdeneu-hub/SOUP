# Which A-Levels Do You Need? Subject Requirements for Medicine, Engineering, Computer Science, Business and Law

Choosing A-levels is really choosing which degrees you can apply for. Some degrees, like **medicine and engineering**, have firm subject requirements that can't be worked around later. Others, like **law and business**, are far more flexible.

This guide gives the typical subject patterns for the most popular degrees, the extra admissions tests to plan for, and the differences between the **UK, Europe, the US and Pakistan**. It's written for international A-level students, especially in **Pakistan and the GCC**.

> **Last reviewed:** 18 November 2026. These are **typical** requirements, not guarantees. Each university and programme sets its own rules, so always check the programme's official page.

## Quick reference

| Degree | Typically required | Often helpful | Extra tests to plan for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Medicine** | Chemistry, and usually Biology | A third science or Maths | UCAT (most UK schools); MDCAT (Pakistan); IMAT (some Italian programmes) |
| **Engineering** | Mathematics, and usually Physics | Further Maths, Chemistry (chemical engineering) | Some universities set their own tests |
| **Computer science** | Mathematics | Further Maths, Physics, Computer Science | Some universities set their own tests |
| **Economics** | Mathematics at many leading universities | Further Maths | Some universities set their own tests |
| **Business / management** | Usually no fixed subjects | Mathematics | Rare |
| **Law** | Usually no fixed subjects | Essay-based subjects (History, English) | LNAT at several leading UK universities |

## Medicine

Medicine has the strictest subject requirements of any common degree.

- **UK:** almost all medical schools require **Chemistry**, and most also require **Biology**. Many expect a third science or Maths. Most UK medical schools use the **UCAT**. The **BMAT has been discontinued**, and universities that used it, including Oxford, Cambridge, Imperial and UCL, now use the UCAT.
- **UK deadline:** for **2027 entry**, applications for most medicine courses must reach UCAS by **15 October 2026 at 18:00 UK time**, so you'll apply with **predicted grades**. [How predicted grades work →](/guides/applying-to-university-with-predicted-grades)
- **Pakistan:** medical colleges require **pre-medical subjects** (Biology, Chemistry and Physics) with **IBCC equivalence** for A-levels, and the **MDCAT** entrance test.
- **Europe:** several countries offer English-taught medicine, for example Italy (often through the **IMAT**) and Greece (NKUA lists an English-taught medical degree). Requirements vary widely, so check each programme. [Studying in Greece →](/guides/study-in-greece-english-taught-programmes)
- **US:** medicine is a **graduate degree**, taken after a four-year bachelor's degree, usually with pre-med science courses.

## Engineering

- **Mathematics is essential** almost everywhere. **Physics** is required for most engineering degrees.
- **Further Mathematics** is valued by top UK engineering departments, and is required by some.
- **Chemistry** is usually needed for chemical engineering.
- **Germany:** A-levels must meet the three-distinct-subjects rule, and Mathematics plus Further Mathematics count as **one** subject for that purpose. [How Europe assesses A-levels →](/guides/study-in-europe-after-a-levels)

## Computer science

- **Mathematics** is required by most universities. **Further Maths** is preferred, and sometimes required, at the most selective ones.
- A-level **Computer Science** is helpful but rarely required. Universities care more about mathematical ability.

## Economics and business

- **Economics** at many leading UK universities requires **Mathematics**, and some prefer Further Maths.
- **Business and management** degrees usually have **no fixed subject requirements**, though Maths strengthens applications to quantitative programmes such as finance and accounting.

## Law

- Most universities set **no required A-levels** for law. Essay-based subjects show the reading and writing skills law needs.
- Several leading UK universities require the **LNAT** (Law National Aptitude Test).
- In the **US**, law is a **graduate degree**, taken after a bachelor's degree.

## Combinations that keep options open

If you're undecided, these combinations keep the most doors open:

- **Maths + Physics + Chemistry:** engineering, physical sciences, computer science, economics, and medicine at some universities.
- **Biology + Chemistry + Maths:** medicine, pharmacy, biomedical sciences, plus many science and quantitative degrees.
- **Maths + Economics + one essay subject:** economics, business, finance, law and social sciences.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which A-levels do I need for medicine?

Most medical schools require Chemistry, and most also require Biology, with a third science or Maths often expected. Most UK medical schools also require the UCAT, and Pakistani medical colleges require the MDCAT and IBCC equivalence.

### Do I need Further Maths for computer science?

Not usually, but it's preferred, and sometimes required, at the most selective universities. Mathematics A-level is required almost everywhere.

### Can I study law without specific A-levels?

Yes, usually. Most universities don't set required subjects for law, but several leading UK universities require the LNAT admissions test.

### Is the BMAT still used?

No. The BMAT has been discontinued, and universities that used it now use the UCAT.
