# Fill It Once: How to Reuse Your Information Across University Application Portals

A typical international applicant doesn't fill in one application. They fill in **five, eight, sometimes fifteen**: UCAS for the UK, the Common App for the US, Studielink and university portals in the Netherlands, Universitaly and university systems in Italy, the Study in Greece platform, and individual university portals everywhere else.

Every one of them asks for the same things: your full name, date of birth, passport number, address, school history, grades, test scores and parents' details. Every one of them wants the same documents uploaded again.

That repetition isn't just tedious. It's **where mistakes happen**: a passport number with one digit wrong, a different spelling of your name, an outdated predicted grade on one form but not another. Admissions officers and visa officers notice inconsistencies.

This guide shows you how to manage multiple applications with **one accurate source of truth**, and how **SOUP Companion** can do the repetitive filling for you.

## Why inconsistencies matter

Small differences between your applications can cause real problems:

- **Identity mismatches.** If the name or passport number on your offer letter doesn't match your passport, you may have to get documents reissued before your visa application.
- **Grade discrepancies.** Different predicted grades on different applications can raise questions when your school's reference arrives.
- **Outdated documents.** Uploading an expired passport scan or an old English test report can delay a decision.
- **Missed fields.** Skipping an optional-looking field that a particular university treats as essential can leave an application incomplete.

## Step 1: Build a master profile before you open any portal

Before you start your first application, collect every piece of information portals commonly ask for in **one place**:

**Personal and identity**
- Full name *exactly* as in your passport, including middle names, plus any previous names
- Date and place of birth, nationality, and any second nationality
- Passport number, issue and expiry dates
- Permanent and correspondence addresses, phone and email

**Family**
- Parents' or guardians' names, occupations and education. US applications often ask for these.

**Education**
- Every school attended, with dates, addresses and curriculum (for example Cambridge International, Edexcel, IB or a national board)
- Subjects, grades achieved and predicted grades
- University details, if you're applying for postgraduate study

**Tests**
- English test, date, overall and component scores, and test reference number
- SAT/ACT/GRE/GMAT scores where relevant

**Activities, achievements and writing**
- Activities, roles, hours per week and achievements
- A base personal statement or motivation letter

**Documents**
- Clear, current PDFs of everything. [See our full application document checklist →](/guides/university-application-document-checklist)

Update the master profile whenever something changes, such as new predicted grades, a renewed passport or a new test score. **Then copy from it, never from memory.**

## Step 2: Track each application separately

For each university, keep a note of:

- the portal and your login;
- the deadline, and whether it's a "received by" or "submitted by" deadline;
- which documents and how many references it needs;
- which personal statement or essay version you used;
- the status: started, submitted, documents pending, decision.

A spreadsheet works. A platform that does it for you works better.

## Step 3: Let software do the copying, and check everything yourself

Copying the same details into ten portals by hand is the slowest and most error-prone part of applying. This is the part SOUP Companion is designed to handle.

## What SOUP Companion does

**SOUP Companion** is a Windows desktop app that connects to your SOUP account. It works like a simple web browser with tabs and an address bar, with a **SOUP assistant panel** beside the page you're on.

When you open an application form inside Companion:

1. **It scans the page and recognises the fields.** Companion identifies common application fields, such as name, date of birth, passport number, address, education and test scores, using its own field dictionary.
2. **It fills what it can from your SOUP profile.** The same information you entered once in SOUP goes into the form, so you aren't retyping it.
3. **It flags gaps and inconsistencies.** The panel shows which fields it filled, which it couldn't fill, and where the information on the page doesn't match your SOUP profile.
4. **It attaches matching documents.** When a form has an upload field, for example for a passport, transcript or English test report, Companion recognises what the field is asking for and attaches the matching file from your SOUP document vault.
5. **It asks when it isn't sure.** If more than one document could fit, for example you have both an IELTS and a TOEFL report, Companion stops and asks you which one to use rather than guessing.
6. **It narrates as it works.** A running activity feed shows what Companion is scanning, filling and attaching, so you can always see what it did.

### What Companion does *not* do

Being clear about this matters:

- **It never submits an application for you.** You review every page and press submit yourself.
- **It doesn't write your essays.** Personal statements and supplementary essays should be your own work and follow each portal's rules.
- **It isn't perfect at recognising every form.** Application portals are built in very different ways. Fields Companion doesn't recognise are left for you to fill in, and anything it fills should be checked. Forms inside embedded frames on some sites may not be detected.
- **It only works on pages you open in Companion.** It doesn't run in the background or browse on its own.

### Who it's for

- **Students** applying to several universities in different countries.
- **School counsellors** helping a whole class through applications.
- **Education consultants** who prepare many applications for many students.

### On a phone or a computer where you can't install software?

SOUP Scholar and SOUP Concierge include a **web Companion**. It's a browser view of your SOUP answers, checklist and documents that you can keep open beside an application, then copy from or drag files across. It works on phones too. It doesn't fill forms automatically, because browsers don't allow one website to edit another.

## A good routine for every application

1. Update your SOUP profile and document vault first.
2. Open the university's portal in Companion and sign in.
3. Let Companion scan and fill each page.
4. **Read every filled field** against your passport and documents.
5. Fill in anything Companion flagged as missing.
6. Confirm any document choices Companion asked you about.
7. Write or paste your essay for this specific university.
8. Review the whole application one last time, then **submit it yourself**.
9. Record the submission in SOUP so your dashboard stays up to date.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it allowed to use autofill tools for university applications?

Filling your own factual details faster is not the same as having someone else write your application. You remain responsible for the accuracy of everything you submit and for following each portal's terms. Essays and personal statements should always be your own work.

### Does SOUP Companion work with UCAS and the Common App?

Companion's new-tab page includes quick links to UCAS and the Common App, and you can browse to any portal. Field recognition works on many forms but not all, so check every page before you submit.

### Is my data safe?

Companion only accesses the pages you open in it and the information in your own SOUP account. It fills forms on your instruction, and you always submit the application yourself.
