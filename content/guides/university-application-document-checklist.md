# The University Application Document Checklist for International Students

Missing documents cause more delays in international admissions than almost anything else. Applications stall because a transcript wasn't stamped, a passport expired halfway through, or an English test score arrived after the deadline.

This checklist covers the documents most universities in the **UK, the US and Europe** ask international applicants for. It also covers the details that trip students up: translations, attestation, file formats and expiry dates.

> Requirements differ by country, university and programme. Use this as your master list, then confirm against each programme's official requirements.

## 1. Identity documents

- **Passport.** It should be valid well beyond your intended start date. Many visa processes require validity for the length of your stay and some months beyond it. If your passport expires within the next two years, consider renewing it before you apply, so the number on your applications matches the one on your visa.
- **Passport-style photographs** in the format each portal or consulate asks for.
- **National ID card**, if your country issues one and a form asks for it.

**Common mistake:** entering your name differently across applications, for example with or without a middle name. Use your name **exactly as it appears in your passport** everywhere.

## 2. Academic records

### For undergraduate applicants

- **Secondary school transcripts or mark sheets** for the final years of school. Many universities want the last two to four years.
- **GCSE/IGCSE or O-level certificates**, where relevant.
- **A-level, IB or national qualification results.** Use final certificates if you have them, or **predicted grades** from your school if you are applying before results.
- **School-leaving certificate or diploma**, when issued.

### For postgraduate applicants

- **Bachelor's degree certificate**, or a letter confirming expected completion.
- **Full university transcripts** listing modules and grades.
- **Grading scale explanation** if your university uses an unusual scale.

**Translations and attestation.** If documents are not in English (or the language of the programme), you will usually need **certified translations**. Some countries and universities also need documents **attested or legalised**, for example through your education board, foreign ministry or an apostille, or recognised by a national body. Allow weeks, not days, for these steps.

## 3. English-language proof

- **IELTS Academic, TOEFL iBT, PTE Academic, Duolingo English Test** or another test the programme accepts.
- **Exemption evidence**, if the programme accepts English-medium schooling or a degree instead of a test. This might be a letter from your school or university.

Check the **minimum overall and component scores**, and **which tests each university accepts**. Not every university accepts every test. Scores also expire, typically after two years.

## 4. Standardised tests (where required)

- **SAT/ACT**: some US universities require these, others are test-optional or test-free. Check each university's current policy.
- **GRE/GMAT**: for some master's and business programmes.
- **Subject-specific tests**: for example, admissions tests for medicine or particular courses in the UK and Europe.

## 5. Personal statements and essays

- **UK (UCAS):** for 2026 entry onwards, the personal statement is answered as **three separate questions**, with a **4,000-character total** including spaces. It goes to all five of your UCAS choices, so don't name a specific university.
- **US (Common App and others):** a main personal essay, plus **supplementary essays** for individual colleges.
- **Europe:** usually a **motivation letter** or **statement of purpose**, written for each programme. Word limits vary.

Keep a **base version** of your statement and adapt it for each programme, rather than writing from scratch every time.

## 6. Recommendations and references

- **UCAS** uses one reference, usually written by your school.
- **US universities** often ask for a **counsellor recommendation** and one or two **teacher recommendations**.
- **European and postgraduate programmes** typically ask for **one or two academic (or professional) references**.

Give recommenders **at least four to six weeks** and a short summary of your achievements and plans. Track who has submitted and who hasn't.

## 7. CV / résumé

Most postgraduate programmes and many European bachelor's programmes ask for a **CV**. Keep it to one or two pages. Include education, achievements, activities, work or volunteering experience, and skills.

## 8. Activities and achievements

For US applications in particular, list **extracurricular activities**. The Common App activities section allows up to ten. Keep evidence ready (certificates, awards, links to work) in case a programme asks for it.

## 9. Portfolio or extra material

Art, design, architecture and some media or music programmes require a **portfolio** or audition. Check format and size limits early.

## 10. Financial documents

- **Bank statements** showing funds for tuition and living costs, often for a specified recent period.
- **Sponsor letter** and the sponsor's bank statements, if a parent or relative is funding you.
- **Scholarship or loan letters**, if relevant.
- **Blocked account confirmation** (Germany) or equivalent proof required for some visas.

Universities may ask for financial evidence at the offer stage, especially in the US, where an I-20 requires it. Immigration authorities usually ask again for the visa.

## 11. After the offer: visa, insurance and accommodation

Once you accept an offer, a second checklist starts:

- **Acceptance letter / CAS / I-20 / pre-enrolment summary**, depending on the country.
- **Visa application documents**, as listed by the embassy or consulate.
- **Health insurance** that meets the visa and university's requirements. [Compare student insurance →](/services/insurance)
- **Accommodation confirmation**, often needed for visas and residence permits. [Browse student accommodation →](/services/accommodation)
- **Medical certificates or tests**, where required.

## File-format tips that save time

- Scan in **colour**, at a readable resolution, as **PDF**.
- Check each portal's **file-size limit** before uploading.
- Use clear file names, such as `Surname_Passport.pdf` or `Surname_Transcript_Grade12.pdf`.
- Keep **one master folder** with the latest version of every document, plus a note of **expiry dates**.
