# A School Counsellor's Guide to Managing University Applications for a Whole Cohort

In a British-curriculum school in **Lahore, Karachi, Dubai, Riyadh or Doha**, one counsellor (or one head of sixth form doing the counsellor's job alongside teaching) may support **dozens of students applying through three or more systems in the same year**: UCAS for the UK, the Common App for the US, and individual portals across Europe and the GCC.

This guide sets out what each system needs from **the school**, a calendar for the year, and practical ways to keep a cohort's applications accurate and on time.

> **Last reviewed:** 18 November 2026. Platform rules change between cycles. Confirm details in each system's adviser documentation.

## What each system needs from the school

### UCAS (UK)

A school registered with UCAS acts as a **centre**. UCAS expects the centre to:

- **monitor, check, advise on and approve** applications linked to the school;
- add **predicted grades** for every pending qualification;
- add a **reference** to every application. A standard school statement can be set up and added to each reference.

Things to know:

- Students **can't see** their predicted grades or reference.
- If you return an application to a student to change their education section, **predicted grades you've already entered are removed**, so finish education details first.
- After sending, **UCAS can't change** the reference or predicted grades.
- UCAS's **Predicted Grades Report** lets schools review the accuracy of past predictions. UCAS doesn't share it with universities.

**2027-entry deadlines:** 15 October 2026 (Oxford, Cambridge and most medicine courses) and the main January 2027 equal-consideration date. Set your **internal deadlines** at least two to three weeks earlier.

### Common App (US)

In the Common App, students invite their **counsellor** and **teachers**. The counsellor submits:

- the **School Report** (with transcript and school profile), plus a **counselor recommendation**;
- later, the **Mid-year Report** and **Final Report**, which can only be sent after the School Report.

Things to know:

- School forms are only sent to colleges **once the student has submitted** their Common App.
- The system **doesn't send reminders** about outstanding forms.
- Outside the US, schools often don't have a "counselor" title. Any appropriate school official (a principal, head of sixth form or teacher) can take on the role.
- Many schools manage this through a platform they subscribe to, which integrates with Common App.

### Europe and the GCC

European and GCC universities rarely have a "school form". Instead, the school's work is **documents**:

- **transcripts** and **predicted grades** on school letterhead;
- **reference letters**, often in a programme-specific format;
- attested or legalised certificates, and for GCC universities, **equivalency** support.

## A counsellor's year

| When | UCAS | Common App | Europe / GCC |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Year 12, spring** | Subject checks for medicine, engineering and Oxbridge | Testing plans (SAT/ACT where needed) | Equivalency and subject-count checks |
| **June–August** | Draft references; agree predicted grades | Students build their activity lists | Shortlists; document checklists |
| **September** | Internal deadline for October applicants | Early Decision/Early Action preparation | Early programme deadlines |
| **15 October** | Oxbridge and medicine deadline | Early deadlines around 1 November | — |
| **November–December** | Main UCAS references and approvals | Regular-decision School Reports | Rolling European applications |
| **January** | Main equal-consideration deadline | Mid-year Reports | Some Dutch programmes close 15 January |
| **February–May** | Track offers; firm and insurance choices | Final decisions | Remaining applications; visa paperwork |
| **August** | Results; confirmation; Clearing support | Final Reports | Final certificates sent |

## Where cohort applications go wrong

1. **Inconsistent student details.** Names spelled differently, passport numbers mistyped, or grades entered differently across portals.
2. **Missing documents.** A transcript or passport scan missing from one application out of eight.
3. **Late subject problems.** Discovering in Year 13 that a student doesn't have the subjects their chosen degree requires.
4. **Recommender bottlenecks.** Teachers asked for ten references in the same week.
5. **No single view.** It's hard to tell which students are ready, which are stuck, and why.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does a school need to do for UCAS applications?

A UCAS centre checks and approves each application, adds predicted grades for pending qualifications, and adds a reference. Students can't see their predicted grades or reference, and UCAS can't change them once the application is sent.

### How do counsellors submit Common App forms?

Students invite their counsellor in the Common App, or through the school's integrated platform. The counsellor submits the School Report, transcript and counsellor recommendation, and later the Mid-year and Final Reports. Forms only reach colleges once the student submits.

### What if our school doesn't have a "counselor"?

Any appropriate school official, such as a principal, head of sixth form or teacher, can take the counsellor role in the Common App and complete the school forms.

### How can counsellors reduce mistakes across many applications?

Keep one accurate record per student and copy from it rather than retyping, track documents and recommenders centrally, and set internal deadlines well before official ones. SOUP and SOUP Companion are designed to do exactly this.
