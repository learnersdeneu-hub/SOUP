# Studying in Greece as an International Student: A Guide to English-Taught Programmes at Public Universities

For most of the last few decades, studying at a Greek public university meant studying in Greek. That has changed. Greek public universities now run a growing number of **English-taught bachelor's and master's programmes for international students**, and the Greek Ministry of Education runs an official platform, **Study in Greece**, to handle applications.

For students from Pakistan, the GCC, Africa and Southeast Asia, Greece offers an EU degree, Schengen-area residence while studying, a Mediterranean cost of living and fees well below many UK and US universities. It is not the right choice for everyone, though. This guide explains how the system works, what to check before applying, and where the common surprises are.

> **Last reviewed:** 17 November 2026. Fees, deadlines and programme lists change every year. Always confirm details on the [official Study in Greece portal](https://apply.studyingreece.edu.gr/en) and the university's own programme page before you apply.

## Why international students are looking at Greece

- **An EU public university degree.** Greek public universities are state institutions within the European Higher Education Area. Their degrees follow the Bologna structure of bachelor's, master's and doctorate.
- **English-taught options at well-known institutions.** These include the National and Kapodistrian University of Athens, the Aristotle University of Thessaloniki, the Athens University of Economics and Business, and the University of Crete.
- **A centralised, official application route.** Non-EU students apply through a government platform rather than through dozens of separate university systems.
- **Living costs.** Outside central Athens in particular, day-to-day costs are typically lower than in the UK, the Netherlands or Germany's big cities.

The trade-off is that **English-taught bachelor's programmes are still a small group**. Sources counted roughly 12–16 English-taught undergraduate programmes at public universities in 2025, mostly in medicine, business, economics, classics and the humanities. There are many more English-taught master's programmes, so Greece is especially strong for postgraduate study.

## How to apply: the Study in Greece platform

Non-EU applicants to English-taught programmes usually apply through **Study in Greece / AtSiG (Apply to Study in Greece)**, the official platform at [apply.studyingreece.edu.gr](https://apply.studyingreece.edu.gr/en). The process generally looks like this:

1. **Create an account** on the Study in Greece platform.
2. **Choose your programmes.** The platform lets undergraduate applicants list several programmes in a single application, up to 20 according to guidance published around the platform.
3. **Upload your documents** (see the checklist below).
4. **Pay any application fee** shown for the programme.
5. **Wait for the university's decision.** Some programmes may interview applicants or ask for more material.
6. **Accept your offer** and receive the acceptance letter you will need for your visa.

Application windows differ by programme and can be short. Check each programme's dates on the portal as soon as it opens for your intake.

### Separate from the Panhellenic exams

Greek nationals and most EU students enter Greek-taught bachelor's programmes through the national **Panhellenic examinations**. International applicants to English-taught programmes do not sit these exams. They apply directly with their school qualifications, which is what makes Greece realistic for students from British-curriculum, IB or national school systems.

## Entry requirements

Requirements are set **programme by programme**, but most undergraduate programmes ask for the following.

### For bachelor's programmes

- **A completed secondary school qualification.** The Study in Greece helpdesk states that most undergraduate programmes accept **A-levels**, the IB, the European or French Baccalaureate, and AP/SAT results as equivalent to a high-school diploma.
- **Transcripts** of your final school years.
- **Proof of English.** IELTS Academic or TOEFL iBT are the most commonly accepted. Published ranges are typically around **IELTS 6.0–6.5 / TOEFL iBT 80–88**, but the programme's own requirement is what counts. Some programmes accept English-medium schooling as proof.
- **Programme-specific requirements.** For example, medicine and science programmes may require particular science subjects.

Some sources say Greek universities may require foreign school certificates to be recognised by **DOATAP**, the Hellenic national recognition centre. Whether and when this applies to non-EU applicants to English-taught programmes varies, so **ask the programme directly** or check its admissions page.

### For master's programmes

- A recognised bachelor's degree in a relevant field, with transcripts.
- Proof of English, as above.
- A CV, a statement of purpose, and usually two recommendation letters.
- For some programmes, work experience or an interview.

## How much does it cost?

There is no single "Greek university fee". Tuition for English-taught programmes is set by each programme, and **non-EU fees are often higher than EU fees**.

- **Greek-taught** undergraduate programmes at public universities are tuition-free for eligible students. This **does not usually apply to English-taught programmes for international students**.
- Third-party summaries report English-taught **bachelor's** fees at public universities mostly in the region of **€4,000–€6,000 a year**, with **medicine considerably higher**. The European Commission's country profile gives a wider non-EU range.
- English-taught **master's** fees vary widely. Many fall in the low thousands of euros for the whole programme, but some are much higher.

**Our advice:** treat any figure you see on a blog, including ours, as a starting point. The fee to budget for is the one on the programme's official page for your intake year and your nationality. SOUP's catalogue shows when each fee was last checked.

You also need to budget for:

- living costs (rent, food, transport, phone);
- health insurance (see below);
- visa and residence permit fees;
- document translation and attestation.

## Student visas and residence permits

If you are not an EU/EEA or Swiss citizen and you will study in Greece full-time, you will generally need:

1. **A national long-stay visa (Type D) for studies.** You apply at the Greek embassy or consulate responsible for your country of residence *before* travelling. A Schengen tourist visa is not enough for study.
2. **A residence permit for studies** after you arrive. University guidance commonly says to apply within a set period after arrival. Check the current deadline with your university's international office.

Documents commonly requested for the visa include:

- your university **acceptance letter**;
- a **valid passport** with enough remaining validity and blank pages;
- the completed **national visa application form** and photos;
- **proof of funds** to cover your stay;
- **health insurance** covering your stay;
- evidence of **accommodation**;
- other documents the consulate lists, which may include attested and translated school certificates.

Processing times vary by consulate and season, and published estimates range from a few weeks to considerably longer. **Apply as early as your acceptance letter allows.** The consulate's own checklist is the final word. SOUP does not give legal or immigration guarantees.

## Health insurance

Proof of health insurance is commonly required for the visa and the residence permit. Check exactly what cover the consulate and university expect, including the minimum coverage, the period, and whether repatriation is included. Then choose a policy that meets those terms.

## Accommodation

Most Greek public universities have **limited student housing**, and international students often rent privately. Practical tips:

- Start looking as soon as you have an offer, especially for Athens and Thessaloniki.
- Budget for a deposit, usually one or two months' rent.
- Ask the university's international office about student residences and trusted landlords.
- Be wary of listings that ask for payment before you have seen a contract.

## Is Greece right for you?

Greece is a strong option if:

- you want an **EU public university degree** at a moderate cost;
- your subject is offered in English (business, economics, medicine, humanities, and many master's fields);
- you are comfortable with a smaller, newer international programme rather than a huge international cohort.

Think carefully if:

- your subject has **no English-taught bachelor's** option in Greece;
- you need a large, established international student support system;
- you are relying on working part-time to fund your studies. Check current work rights on your residence permit.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I study in Greece in English?

Yes. A growing number of bachelor's and many master's programmes at Greek public universities are taught entirely in English. Most other programmes are still taught in Greek, so check the language of instruction for each programme.

### Is university free in Greece for international students?

Greek-taught undergraduate programmes at public universities are tuition-free for eligible students. English-taught programmes for international students generally charge tuition, and non-EU fees are often higher than EU fees.

### Can I apply to Greek universities with A-levels?

Generally, yes. The Study in Greece helpdesk states that most undergraduate programmes accept A-levels as equivalent to a high-school diploma. The specific grades and subjects depend on the programme.

### Do I need to speak Greek?

Not for an English-taught programme. Learning some Greek will make daily life, part-time work and housing much easier, and many universities offer Greek language courses.

### Do I need a visa to study in Greece?

Non-EU/EEA students staying longer than 90 days generally need a national long-stay (Type D) visa for studies, followed by a residence permit after arrival.
