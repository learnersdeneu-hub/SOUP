# Studying Abroad From the Gulf: A Guide for British-Curriculum Students and Their Families

The Gulf has some of the world's largest communities of **British-curriculum students**. Emirati, Saudi, Qatari and other Gulf nationals study IGCSEs and A-levels alongside expatriate families from Pakistan, India, the UK and beyond. Their options after school are unusually wide: universities **at home in the GCC**, the **UK**, **Europe**, the **US**, or their family's home country.

That choice is a real advantage, but it also means more systems, deadlines and rules to manage. This guide sets out the main destinations, what GCC families should check that others might not, and a realistic timeline.

> **Last reviewed:** 18 November 2026. Scholarship, visa and equivalency rules change. Check the official sources for your situation.

## Your main options

### 1. Universities in the GCC

Universities in the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar and elsewhere accept British-curriculum students, and the UAE in particular has grown as a destination for students from across the world.

- **Key requirement:** most require an **equivalency certificate** for your IGCSE/A-level qualifications from the relevant ministry. In the UAE that's the Ministry of Education, and universities often add their own subject counts and minimum grades. [UAE equivalency explained →](/guides/what-to-do-after-o-levels)
- **Best for:** families who want to stay close to home, or students with sponsorship tied to local universities.

### 2. The United Kingdom

The UK is a natural fit for A-level students, and recent reporting suggests more Gulf students have been choosing the UK over the US.

- **Applications** go through **UCAS**, with up to five choices and one personal statement.
- **Deadlines for 2027 entry:** 15 October 2026 for Oxford, Cambridge and most medicine courses. The main equal-consideration deadline is in January 2027.
- [How predicted grades and UCAS offers work →](/guides/applying-to-university-with-predicted-grades)

### 3. Europe

Europe offers English-taught degrees at public universities with tuition often well below the UK and US. That includes **Germany** (where most public universities charge no tuition for most programmes), the **Netherlands**, **Italy** and **Greece**.

- **Key requirement:** each country checks your A-levels against its own entry rules. Germany, for example, requires three A-levels in distinct subjects.
- [Studying in Europe after A-levels →](/guides/study-in-europe-after-a-levels) · [Studying in Greece →](/guides/study-in-greece-english-taught-programmes)

### 4. The United States

US universities use the **Common App** (and other platforms) and look at your whole profile: transcript, counsellor report, recommendations, essays, activities and, where required, SAT/ACT.

- **Best for:** students who want a flexible, broad first degree, or a liberal-arts education.

## What GCC families should check that others might not

### Equivalency, even if you study abroad

If you may **return home for work or postgraduate study**, check whether your **foreign degree** will need recognition by your home ministry, and whether your **school certificates** need equivalency first. Ask early, because sorting this out afterwards can be slow.

### Government sponsorship rules

If you hope to study with **government sponsorship**, check your sponsor's **approved universities and programmes list before you apply**. These lists change, and recent reporting noted changes to the UAE's approved list for state scholarships in 2026. An offer from a university that isn't on the list may not be funded.

### Subject choices for medicine and engineering

Gulf universities and international ones both expect **science and maths A-levels** for medicine, pharmacy and engineering, and some UAE universities don't count Arabic or Islamic Education towards their subject totals. [A-level requirements by degree →](/guides/a-level-subjects-for-medicine-engineering-business-law)

### Expatriate students: think about three systems

If you're a **Pakistani or Indian student in the Gulf**, you may want to keep **home-country universities** open too. For Pakistan, that means checking **IBCC equivalence** rules, which differ for students who sat exams overseas. [IBCC equivalence →](/guides/what-to-do-after-o-levels)

### Visas, insurance and accommodation

Every destination outside the GCC means a **student visa**, **health insurance** that meets the visa's rules, and **accommodation**, often arranged months in advance.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I study in the UK from the UAE with A-levels?

Yes. UK universities accept A-levels taken in the UAE in the same way as A-levels taken in the UK. You apply through UCAS with your school's predicted grades and reference.

### Do I need UAE equivalency if I study abroad?

You need Ministry of Education equivalency to enter UAE universities. If you study abroad, check separately whether your foreign degree will need recognition when you return. This matters especially for government jobs and regulated professions.

### Which is better for Gulf students: the UK, Europe or the US?

It depends on your subject, budget, sponsorship and career plans. The UK suits A-level students who know their subject. Europe offers lower-cost English-taught degrees. The US offers a broader, more flexible first degree at a higher cost.

### Can expatriate students in the Gulf apply to universities in Pakistan or India?

Yes, but they must meet that country's equivalence rules. For Pakistan, IBCC equivalence rules for students who studied overseas differ from those for students who studied in Pakistan.
